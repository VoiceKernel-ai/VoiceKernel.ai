Carriers, energy and water

Outages are a communication problem before they are an engineering one.

When something breaks, the call volume arrives all at once and every caller wants the same three facts. Meanwhile the connection, move and billing queues do not pause.

An illustrative reference architecture, not a customer case study. No organisation is named and no deployment is described - this is what the layers look like for a telco & utilities organisation, so you can map it onto your own estate.

Only the bottom layer is specific to telco & utilities. The three above it are identical in every deployment, which is the point: the voice layer does not need to know what industry it is in, only which systems it is allowed to reach and what it may say.

What the agent actually does

Outage notifications

Outbound at scale with the current restoration estimate, and inbound answering the same from the same source.

Billing enquiries

Explain a bill line by line against usage, and take a payment arrangement.

Connections and moves

Book the appointment, confirm the address, and write it back to field service.

What keeps it deployable

Do-not-call enforcement

Washed before dialling, with the result recorded rather than assumed.

Hardship handling

Distress detection with immediate human transfer.

Cost control

Budget enforced at campaign creation, so a runaway outage broadcast is impossible.

An outage turns a steady inbound queue into a spike in minutes, which is exactly when hold times damage trust most.
